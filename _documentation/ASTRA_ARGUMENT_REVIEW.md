# Astra argument review — current constitutive account

The current corpus defends the Operator's stronger thesis: **human interpersonal moral standards are constituted by adequate justification to those affected, under defended conditions of standing and reciprocity, with personal judgment and accountability**. It does not retreat to the weaker claim that discussion is useful, and it does not claim that empirical findings have settled metaethics.

Runtime edition: `morarg-argument-4`. There are 12 rooms, 39 main steps, 14 conditional continuations, 212 authored choices, 36 selectable concerns, 41 source entries, three full case rooms and ten objection alcoves. The opening contains six exhibits and six main questions, including three of each in the named **Good and bad** section. Thirteen particular choices receive inference/evidence review. Five separate rules identify possible incompatibilities between narrowly scoped current answers. The new authority and semantics content adds no choice-level flags or consistency rules.

## The positive case and its remaining burden

A demand imposed on another life claims more than its author's preference or power. The account defends reciprocal answerability because comparable interests need a relevant difference, and an author's identity or dominance alone does not answer the affected person's complaint. This supports standing before bargaining power, criticism of consensus and a dissenter's possible vindication before victory. Needs, particular responsibilities and circumstances can justify different treatment; equal standing does not demand equal outcomes.

The further premise is sufficiency: adequately meeting these justificatory demands constitutes an interpersonal standard's validity. The positive relational explanation connects owed treatment, standing to demand, accountability and repair, correction of accepted rules, and legitimate selection among permissible arrangements. These are substantive explanatory gains over a bare statement of preference or power. They are not shown to be unique gains over the strongest relational realist, who can preserve the same relations while grounding their authority in independent reasons. The choice of sufficient relational grounding remains a defended normative-explanatory commitment. No additional first-order complaint is required of that realist. [AUTHORITY_ARGUMENT.md](AUTHORITY_ARGUMENT.md) makes the inference, premises and failure conditions explicit.

Equal standing receives an interpersonal defence, but the argument does not demonstrate that its authority excludes every independent explanation. Local constitution can therefore coexist with realism about basic reasons. The broader antirealist ambition remains open. A counterfactual justification relation is also distinct from an actual promise or decision: those normative powers can do constitutive work within morality without grounding every constraint on their legitimacy. People can discover what a constructed standard requires; “construction” and “discovery” do not name mutually exclusive activities at every level.

Adequacy is no longer an unexplained success word. The exhibit states evidence, alternatives, representation, coercion, challenge, burden comparison and revision conditions before the verdict. Their substantive standing and weighting premises can themselves be challenged. The severe-burden/trivial-benefit hypothetical tests formal procedural inclusion without granting that its weighting is adequate. A credible surviving wrong must be able to force revision of sufficiency or scope, rather than being automatically reclassified as a procedural defect.

The deeper primary review materially sharpens the opponent. Scanlon permits construction of interpersonal right and wrong while retaining prior reasons; a procedure's role in justifying belief does not establish its role in making a standard true. Enoch challenges the automatic authority of agency's constitutive features, and distinguishes a global construction of reasons from a local one. Rawls supplies a politically scoped model of substantive constraints, not a universal moral truthmaker. Street permits error within a wider evaluative standpoint without making every current endorsement self-validating. Exact locators and access limits are in [PRIMARY_REVIEW.md](PRIMARY_REVIEW.md).

The remaining philosophical work is specific: defend standing against a coherent hierarchical alternative; explain constitutive sufficiency against discovery and prior-reasons accounts; compare conflicting justified claims without a concealed metric; justify proxy accountability, future-person and nonhuman scope; and defend legitimate action when reasonable disagreement persists. Naming these burdens does not cancel the positive argument, but claiming them solved would misrepresent its current reach.

## What a visitor can now test

The opening's dedicated evaluative-language section distinguishes a purpose's successful pursuit, a person's welfare and moral worth. It distinguishes a subjective act of judging from truth depending on that judge's approval, and conviction from warrant. Its affirmative bridge is that a demand governing affected people must answer their comparable claims. The premise is defended rather than smuggled into “good.” Geach, Moore, Hume and Railton supply contrasting primary context. The main steps `good-senses`, `good-ground` and `good-certainty` each give four unflagged options, with separate burdens for intersubjective, realist, individualist and undecided views and for different ways of testing conviction. Emotion is neither an automatic error nor proof.

The final rule is the cross-room check; the others compare commitments about a fixed case. All are presented as possible inconsistencies between visible current answers, never diagnoses of a visitor. A changed-mind/distinction acknowledgement records an explanation and leaves the active pair visible. Revising an outdated answer recomputes it. Inactive or stale conditional answers cannot supply a current conflict, and reactivation requires reconfirmation.

Negative controls matter as much as positive examples. Realism can coexist with public justification; a visitor can change their philosophical position; general desert does not dictate every marginal sentence; concern for present needs does not conflict with concern for future people; an exception can be permitted without being required; emotion does not make reasoning irrational. These do not generate cross-response flags. No free-text interpretation or inference about motives occurs.
