# Why reciprocal justification might constitute moral authority

> **Historical (editions 1–4).** Edition 5 (build 0.6.0) replaces the constitutive thesis with the claim that objective moral facts are not needed. See `ARGUMENT_STRUCTURE.md` and `EDITION5_REBUILD.md`.

Argument marker: `ts_ms=1793124958452`. This is MORARG's defended account of human interpersonal moral standards. It is a substantive, defeasible grounding argument, not a definition that makes a metaphysical conclusion true.

## The conclusion and its target

**Adequate reciprocal justification constitutes the authority of a valid interpersonal moral demand.** It does not merely help an observer predict which demand has authority from elsewhere. People can discover what such a justified relation requires, so construction and discovery of particular answers are compatible. The disputed issue is what makes the answer authoritative.

This claim concerns what people may demand of one another and what they owe one another. It does not establish that every reason, value or evaluative truth is constructed. Nor does an actual meeting, majority vote or expression of approval create validity. The relevant relation may obtain before anyone recognises it.

## The argument

1. **An obligation to someone is more than a reason to produce an outcome.** A person can have excellent advice without a right to command the advisee. A person wronged can be owed an answer, repair or restraint in a way a spectator with the same prediction is not. This distinction is supported by cases involving authority and accountability, rather than stipulated by renaming every reason a demand. It motivates a relational explanandum: why this person can claim this treatment from that person.

2. **Comparable affected interests and agency deserve equal justificatory standing.** Neither ancestry, power nor the identity of the claimant alone warrants discounting an otherwise comparable burden. Need, responsibility and circumstance can make relevant differences. The defence is that a demand claiming authority over another life must answer that person's claim rather than simply replace it with its author's purposes. This is an explicit moral commitment. The person who claims comparable protection while denying it without a relevant distinction faces a challenge to their own demand; that is not a deduction that every possible agent accepts equality.

3. **The content of a demand must be justified within that relation.** Accurate prediction of a beneficial outcome is insufficient if the demand excludes a burdened person from standing or treats another's agency as disposable. Each relevant claim must be considered against feasible alternatives under the independently stated conditions below. Actual approval can fail these conditions; a correct complaint can satisfy them while universally rejected. The target is answerability, not unanimous willingness.

4. **The proposed ground fits the authority being explained.** Reciprocal justifiability is a relation between people specifying who may require what of whom, who is owed an answer or repair, and why a rejected complaint matters. Treating that relation as constitutive connects a demand's content with its authority rather than placing its ground beyond those justified relations. The same framework explains correction through a newly recognised burden, and permits legitimate agency to specify obligations where several arrangements are defensible. This gives the proposed explanation positive fit and scope. It does not yet establish a comparative advantage over a realist who explains all those relations equally well.

5. **That warrant supports a proposal, not a forced ranking of the rivals.** The claim that the relation supplies sufficient grounds is a further normative-explanatory commitment. It does not follow from relational vocabulary, usefulness, consensus, a lack of extra predictions, or counting fewer entities. We favour this constitutive framework for its integrated account of owed treatment, answerability, repair and legitimate human authorship. A realist can retain the same structure while grounding its authority in independent normative truths. The present cases support the framework's explanatory fit; they do not show that the strongest realist leaves one of these phenomena unexplained. Its further preference for relational grounding is candidly a defended interpretive commitment, not an established abductive victory.

6. **Therefore, given standing and the grounding premise, valid interpersonal standards are constituted through reciprocal justification.** This is stronger than a consultation method. It explains why an accepted demand can lack authority and why an unheard complaint can defeat it. It does not claim to derive the normative starting points from descriptive facts, eliminate personal judgment, guarantee a unique answer, or refute every form of moral realism.

Premises 2 and 5 carry the main burden. Premises 1 and 3 constrain the proposal, but do not deductively establish 5. A reader can understand the argument and reasonably dispute either premise.

## What counts as adequate: conditions stated before the verdict

These indicators constrain inquiry without defining an adequate answer as whatever MORARG already considers right:

- Identify all materially affected people and serious indirect effects; do not restrict the circle to voters, bargainers or present speakers.
- Check relevant facts and uncertainty, including whether the claimed alternatives and costs are real.
- Compare feasible alternatives, including inaction or delay, against severity, probability, reversibility, agency, existing responsibilities and the distribution of burdens.
- Allow meaningful challenge to both the evidence and the standard; examine coercion, retaliation, inaccessible information and control of the procedure.
- Support direct communication; otherwise distinguish known wishes, welfare evidence and prospective interests, disclose proxy conflicts and enable independent challenge.
- Explain relevant differences between claims, including why a serious interest may be burdened. Require the same explanation when positions are exchanged.
- State what could defeat the conclusion and preserve correction, including repair of a burden already imposed.

These conditions are substantial but incomplete. Equal standing and judgments of relevance and weight remain normative commitments, not outputs of a neutral machine. They may be challenged independently. The presumption against sacrificing grave interests for minor convenience is defended as one such judgment. A visitor can reject it. For example, if a fully informed and inclusive process permits imposing a severe bodily burden on one person for many trivial conveniences, procedural inclusion has not answered the objection to that weighting. The author must defend or change the weighting rule; labelling the process inadequate without naming a substantive reason would evade the test. The completeness condition cannot immunise the theory: a credible wrong that survives the stated tests challenges their sufficiency.

## The strongest realist countermodel

Consider a realist who accepts every affected interest, agency condition, justified complaint, proxy safeguard and correction in this account. They condemn the same killing, protect the same participant and reject the same exclusion. They say independent normative truths explain **why** these considerations count and why reciprocal answerability has authority. They need add no new practical verdict or complaint. Their position is not refuted by saying the procedure already works.

A naturalist can seek that grounding in informed interests and social relations. A reasons realist can accept local contractual construction while holding that the reasons selecting its conditions are underived. A relational realist can even accept that some interpersonal obligations are constituted by relationships while remaining realist about the principles making those relationships authoritative. This last view overlaps the local conclusion instead of serving as a merely discovery-based rival.

MORARG therefore withdraws the shortcut that a different foundation must supply an additional first-order reason. The disagreement concerns explanatory priority. Its own preference is for a reciprocal account of standing and obligation grounded in the claims of vulnerable, acting people. That preference supplies an affirmative account; it is not evidence that independent normative truths are impossible.

## A limited generative case, and why it is limited

Suppose two equally defensible allocations remain after the relevant burdens are compared. A justified decision procedure may select one and establish an obligation to comply, subject to its limits. The decision can help determine what is now owed rather than discover a unique allocation that was mandatory beforehand. This shows why practical agency can do constitutive work within morality.

It does not establish the whole theory. Actual promises, consent and legitimate decisions have normative effects only under conditions that themselves need justification. A realist can recognise those powers. Moreover, a counterfactual justification relation is not an actual agreement. The generative example supports an architecture combining constraints and human authorship; it cannot by itself turn every basic moral constraint into a product of choice.

## Underived commitments and the antirealist ambition

Equal standing, the significance of affected interests and reciprocal limits on imposing burdens are not derived here from nonmoral observations. MORARG argues for them within the standpoint of people whose lives and agency are at issue. Their warrant comes from comparing claims and rejecting unearned authority, rather than appealing to current popularity or an individual's passing approval.

This is an intersubjective defence of those commitments. It does **not** yet demonstrate that their authority could have no stance-independent explanation. If such an explanation is required, the resulting view may be a hybrid: local constitution supported by realist reasons. The current argument gives a positive local constitutive thesis and a reasoned preference for a relational foundation; it leaves the broader antirealist ambition unproved. Claiming otherwise would exceed the argument, not strengthen it.

## Hard tests and failure conditions

| Test | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| A fully informed adult accepts permanent subordination | Approval alone does not settle the issue. A prohibition must name a substantive agency/standing limit—such as irrevocably removing future challenge rights—and defend it against the value of the adult's present choice. The answer cannot be hidden in the word “adequate.” |
| A coherent hierarchy rejects equal standing | Show what allegedly makes the rank relevant and whether the same standard can justify the burden to those below it. The dispute is substantive; coherence alone neither validates nor refutes it. A hierarchy that never claims equal protection is not caught by a fabricated inconsistency. |
| A powerful refuser understands every complaint | Distinguish a claim's validity, the agent's motivation and others' reasons to resist. Neither construction nor realism wins merely because the refuser remains unmoved. |
| Two incompatible arrangements both survive | They may be permissible alternatives rather than simultaneously mandatory. A justified selection procedure needs its own defence. If the theory makes contradictory requirements mandatory, its conditions or application fail. |
| A credible wrong survives every stated condition | Treat it as a counterexample to sufficiency or scope, not automatically as a hidden procedural defect. Identify any revision openly and test it independently. |
| A third party or non-speaking person was omitted | This is a failure to meet the stated inclusion condition. Repair the inquiry; do not count the case as evidence that genuine sufficiency has been demonstrated. |
| Everyone endorses cruelty, or a correct outsider condemns it | Endorsement does not decide validity and outsider status does not disqualify a complaint. Identify the substantive standing, agency or burden condition at stake, including any adaptive endorsement; if none can be defended, consensus rejection is not justified by assertion alone. These cases test approval-independence, not a unique advantage over realism. |
| A realist grants all practical reasons and relations | Compare grounds directly. No extra first-order objection is required for a realist to remain a serious rival. |

## Primary support and limits

[Darwall's 2009 chapter manuscript](https://law.yale.edu/sites/default/files/documents/pdf/Intellectual_Life/ltw_darwall.pdf), selected pp. 1–7 and 13–16, distinguishes practical authority and accountability from epistemic expertise or beneficial compliance. Its interdefined authority concepts do not independently prove that everyone has equal standing. The [Cambridge record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/reasons-for-action/authority-and-secondpersonal-reasons-for-acting/68D36ABB9AADBE5EDCBEE2A5924E5D4B) verifies the publication; the entire 2006 book was not reviewed.

[Forst's 2016 paper](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/archive/2016/01/Forst-Justification-of-Basic-Rights-Final.pdf), selected §§1–2, manuscript pp. 1–9, joins equal normative standing to reciprocal justification in a theory of basic legal rights. Membership is expressly bracketed. [Selected passages of his 2012 book](https://eltalondeaquiles.pucp.edu.pe/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Rainer_Forst_Jeffrey_Flynn_The_Right_to_JustifiBookFi-org_.pdf), printed pp. 5–6, 35–39 and 53–55, locate moral responsibility in relations to others while retaining a substantive moral starting point. Neither source supplies a premise-free deduction or proves MORARG's broader metaethics.

The existing primary treatments of Scanlon, Enoch, Railton, Rawls and Street remain essential opposing and comparison sources. Their access descriptions are in [PRIMARY_REVIEW.md](PRIMARY_REVIEW.md). MORARG's numbered argument is an authored synthesis, not attributed wholesale to any of these philosophers.
