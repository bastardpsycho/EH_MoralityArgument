# A decision-mapping studio

The useful promise is: **make the assumptions behind a judgement discussable**.
A visitor chooses a situation, changes one condition, and compares the structure of
the same starting judgement. This is useful for explaining a mechanism, preparing
a discussion, teaching model literacy, or generating a question for later inquiry.

The visitor should be able to say:

1. What changed in the situation?
2. Did the model shift who counts, what binds, how the judgement is processed, or
   which values are emphasized?
3. Which stipulated forces changed, and what assumption would I challenge?

The guided workspace holds starting conditions, simulated traits, horizon and random
draws constant across two arms. It changes one named lever. Outcome cards and a
plain table carry the explanation; the 3D map is an additional spatial view. The
download retains the assumptions and numerical outputs so a pretty chart does not
become detached from its setup.

The full model is the default opening workspace, showing the vector field with
cohort traces, octant names and finite-horizon endpoint clusters. Guided exploration
remains a secondary workspace for paired comparisons. The full model is an
independent sandbox with all original cases, levers,
trait inputs, two probes, local influences, population animation, field overlays
and fixed-attractor experiments. Switching workspaces does not silently overwrite
either workspace's controls.

## Presentation choices

The observatory theme uses a midnight navy canvas and layered navy panels, with
cyan actions and baseline paths, pink changed-condition paths, and violet accents.
A serif headline introduces the idea; system sans-serif type carries controls and
data. The baseline and changed conditions have distinct labels and line styles as
well as colours. Chart surfaces, hover labels and full-model controls share the
dark palette. Keyboard focus remains visible and reduced-motion settings are respected.
Both workspace containers expand to 3600 CSS pixels. At viewport widths of at least
2300 CSS pixels, the charts reserve at least 1600 pixels of plotting height after
Plotly margins. Smaller viewports retain responsive chart sizing.
Small screens stack the controls and results; the numerical reading does not
require a 3D gesture. Advanced assumptions are available without blocking first use.

The source's real-case examples are retained with expandable context. Literature
tags describe inherited annotations; they are not confidence grades for a forecast.
The app maps decision configurations, not whether someone complies, helps, harms,
or arrives at a specific policy conclusion.

## A consumer presentation

Open with everyday life. Ask what scarcity might do to the *structure* of a decision.
Show the starting and changed conditions, then read the four shifts. Reset to show
that unchanged conditions produce no difference. Switch to the disaster case and
adjust shared fate to demonstrate that one force can interact with another.

End by asking: "Which assumption would you change, and what observation would make
you reconsider it?" A compelling presentation makes the mechanism inspectable;
it does not promise a machine that settles a moral dispute.

## Future product tests

Observe a few new users trying to explain the comparison without guidance. Measure
whether they can identify the changed input, distinguish configuration from verdict,
and understand that the numbers are illustrative. Only then expand to custom case
authoring, shareable saved sessions, or a facilitated classroom/workshop mode.
Public hosting, accounts, personal profiling and empirical calibration are separate
product decisions, not prerequisites for this demonstrator.
