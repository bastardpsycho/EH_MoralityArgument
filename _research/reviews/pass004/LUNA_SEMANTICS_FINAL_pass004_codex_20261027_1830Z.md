## Verdict

No material correction remains in the saved semantics section. It does not infer that moral judgments are individual preferences merely because people make them, and it does not claim that certainty makes a judgment objective. It distinguishes instrumental success, welfare, and moral evaluation, then advances equal standing as an explicit normative premise. The constitutive account is presented as a defended proposal whose sufficiency remains open to challenge.

## Checks

- **`good-standards` / `good-senses`:** The three uses of “good” are distinct and the examples do not make success at a task equivalent to moral goodness. Geach’s attributive account supports the type-relative distinction and rejection of commendation-only analysis; the text correctly says that Geach does not settle moral authority. Moore’s §6 supplies a serious semantic counterexample to deriving a moral theory from word meaning, without being treated as a refutation of naturalism. See [Geach, “Good and Evil,” ¶¶1–3, 8–12](https://fair-use.org/peter-t-geach/good-and-evil) and [Moore, *Principia Ethica*, ch. I, §§5–10](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/53430/53430-h/53430-h.htm).
- **`good-judgment` / `good-ground`:** The copy separates the act of judging from what makes a judgment true. It labels equal standing and reciprocal justification as moral commitments, and asks the preferred view to defend why sufficient justification constitutes validity. The realist option is fair: a naturalist may ground truth in human interests while denying that present approval makes it true. Shared, realist, individualist, and open answers remain unflagged.
- **`good-certainty`:** Confidence and emotion are treated as relevant to inquiry but insufficient to establish or defeat a claim. Hume’s general standpoint is accurately presented as a model for correcting proximity and private advantage, then explicitly limited to a precedent rather than proof of MORARG’s grounding thesis. The source locator includes the cited T 3.3.1.15–18 passage. See [Hume, *Treatise* 3.3.1](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/3/1).

## Optional precision

In `objective-senses` and `good-ground`, “independent of our evaluative approval” could be narrowed to “not made true by actual or present evaluative approval.” That would mirror the later Railton feedback and leave explicit room for realist accounts involving idealized interests. The current wording is defensible in context and does not create a review blocker.
