# Final independent authority and semantics review

This is a targeted review of the saved pass004 argument and authored choices, not a visitor study or an independent full-source review. It follows the initial critique in the separate TERRA_AUTHORITY_REVIEW file.

## Findings addressed

The initial argument risked requiring a realist to supply an extra practical verdict. The saved account now presents a realist who accepts the very same interests, complaints, duties and outcome, but grounds their authority in independent reasons. The new `path-authority-ground` asks explicitly what makes the same verdict valid and offers constitutive, realist, hybrid and undecided answers. It does not grade these alternatives as reasoning failures. The account distinguishes a procedure that constructs an interpersonal standard from the discovery of its implications, and explicitly allows local construction alongside realist prior reasons.

The revised positive case links standing, owed answers, repair, correction and legitimate human agency in an explanatory proposal. It presents the sufficiency of justified relations as a contested inference, not a result compelled by the relational form of obligation, empirical success or a missing extra objection. The final room preserves a possible wrong surviving the proposed tests as a challenge to sufficiency; it does not automatically redefine the counterexample as a procedural failure. The strongest realist alternative and the prior-reasons hybrid remain available. The misleading phrase that might have implied the realist treats people as merely evidence is absent from the saved room copy.

The `path-authority-defeater` exposes a difficult trade-off: severe bodily burden versus many trivial conveniences after formal participation safeguards. It separates the practical burden from a further question about its ground, and acknowledges that a realist need not invent an extra complaint. The objection about historical reform distinguishes warranted criticism from actual social acceptance; it does not claim that agreement resolves the metaphysical issue.

The added opening distinguishes instrumental good, benefit to a person, and moral evaluation. It distinguishes a person's act of judging from the conditions of truth, and certainty from warrant, without dismissing emotional experience. An intersubjective grounding is argued for as a further thesis; it is not made true by a dictionary definition of “good” or “subjective.” The semantic response choices preserve individual subjectivism, realism, the proposed framework and uncertainty without marking any as an error merely for its position.

## Remaining disagreement and limits

The decisive open issue is whether reciprocal justifiability is sufficient as a ground of interpersonal validity, or whether independent reasons explain that relation's authority. Explanatory integration is a reason to prefer an account only in a comparative evaluation; a realist can explain the same relational features. Equal standing, the relevance and weight of claims, and representation of absent or non-speaking interests remain substantive premises. An unconvinced powerful agent's lack of motivation does not decide whether a moral reason applies or establish it from premises that agent accepts.

Even unanimous endorsement—including a victim's apparent approval—does not settle the proposed idealized standard. Applying it to an accepted cruel norm depends on a defensible standing and burden criterion; that case is compatible with a realist interpretation too. A correct outsider can expose a neglected claim without constructing the standard through their actual participation. Neither example independently discriminates the metaphysical accounts. When weighty complaints conflict, the outlined comparison is a discipline, not a complete metric, and a legitimate decision can leave a real moral remainder.

The claims are presented as an authored philosophical proposal, not an empirical proof or settled resolution of metaethics. Fairness research, historical abuses and policy cases inform facts or illustrate tests; they do not decide the constitutive grounding question. The evidence base's published access limits remain material.
