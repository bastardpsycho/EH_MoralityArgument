# Independent challenge to constitutive authority

Scope: analytical review of pass003 content and `_documentation/PRIMARY_REVIEW.md`, not a fresh full-text literature review or user study. The proposed thesis is that adequate interpersonal justification constitutes validity, not only that discussion is useful. It explicitly allows prior reasons and defends equal standing; it does not claim a procedure produces all normativity from nothing.

## The strongest rival and the decisive inference

A realist can grant that every affected interest has been identified, every objection answered and every relevant practical reason accounted for. They need not assert an extra first-order reason or reach a different conclusion. They can say that independently valid normative reasons explain why those answers are adequate. Thus the statement that a further *verdict* must contribute another relevant reason risks mistaking a dispute about the ground of validity for a request for another practical consideration. Agreement that the practical work is complete does not itself establish which account grounds its validity.

The constructivist's strongest response is not merely that there is no extra reason on the list: a moral demand aimed at governing relationships owes its authority to an adequate answer to the claims of those it burdens. Treating that answerability as the constitutive role of interpersonal right avoids positing a further source of interpersonal validity to explain a distinction for which the adequate relation already does the work. This is a substantive explanatory and normative inference, not a logical consequence of shared practice or a proof that an independent ground cannot exist. A realist can say a ground explains adequacy without adding a further complaint. Explanatory economy is a comparative consideration only if both explanations explain the relevant facts equally well; the sufficiency and role premises must be defended, not hidden in “adequate.” The relationship with prior reasons must remain explicit: a local constructive account can depend on reasons it does not construct, and therefore does not by itself establish global antirealism.

## Pressure tests

1. **Universally accepted cruelty:** Suppose every existing person, including the victim, sincerely endorses an inherited rule that causes severe unnecessary suffering. Actual consensus is not the proposed test. The account can condemn the rule if its idealized criteria of equal standing and relevant burdens support a complaint even when no one voices it. But that result depends on those substantive criteria, and a realist can reach the same result by independent reasons. If “adequate” simply means “not cruel,” the explanatory step is circular.
2. **Coherent hierarchy:** An informed, internally coherent hereditary hierarchy treats caste as decisive and claims its stable social order benefits everyone. The response must explain why origin alone cannot justify discounting a comparable person's agency or severe burden, and why concrete differences in need, responsibility and role are not equivalent to inherited rank. Internal coherence and sincerity alone decide neither system. Compare a specific trade-off rather than making the hierarchy omniscient and harmless.
3. **Unmoved refuser:** A powerful aggressor understands the victim's complaint and can safely refuse reciprocity. The account can still affirm the victim's standing and others' reasons to protect them; the fact of refusal does not settle whether a reason applies. It does not by itself prove an authority that binds an agent who rejects every relevant starting commitment. Distinguish motivational failure, a claim of authority and grounds for proportional protection.
4. **Correct outsider:** A distant critic who has never belonged to the community exposes an overlooked burden and a rule's inconsistency with defensible standing. Under the proposal the criticism may be correct without participation, recognition or eventual success. A realist can explain the same correction as discovering independently valid reasons. The case tests dependence on actual approval and epistemic openness, not the metaphysical distinction on its own.
5. **Prior reasons:** If reasons for recognising persons, facts about harm, or why certain comparisons matter are prior to the interpersonal standard, say which work those reasons perform. Scanlon's local construction can be compatible with prior normative reasons. Calling the result constituted does not automatically make all its inputs constructed or answer whether those inputs are stance independent.
6. **Competing claims:** Two people suffer serious but different harms, only one can be prevented, and a simple role reversal yields no clear ranking. Show which considerations matter—severity, likelihood, agency, special obligations and availability of alternatives—without presenting them as a proven formula. If several outcomes survive, distinguish a justified decision process from a demonstration that the losing complaint was answered away. A realist can endorse the same practice.

## Visitor diagnostic and feedback

Give both accounts the identical facts, victim interests, safeguards and moral verdict. Ask what makes the verdict valid: (a) adequate interpersonal justification constitutes the standard; (b) prior reasons make the justification correct; (c) the evidence and verdict do not decide; (d) another account. Each is a substantive option, not a factual quiz or flag. Feedback should name the selected interpretation and a real burden for it. Explicitly permit a visitor to hold prior reasons together with a locally constructed interpersonal standard; do not group that position with simplistic majority approval or global construction.

The branch answers and checks should track the exact hypothetical and selected words. A contradiction rule should not flag a change of mind as if both claims remain actively endorsed without offering revision and explanation. Personal values, realist commitments, skepticism and an unresolved authority dispute are not automatically inconsistent. Shared conclusions and psychological evidence do not demonstrate that either metaphysical account is true.
